# Bucket 11 — Acoustics and timbre literature

Read `.agents/round2/BRIEF.md` first. It defines the three-round method, the axis
model and the output structure. Follow it exactly.

## What this bucket must cover

- Thomas Rossing, Science of Percussion Instruments, and his papers on cymbal and drum modes - the physical basis for why bow, edge and bell sound different.
- Neville Fletcher and Rossing, The Physics of Musical Instruments - percussion chapters.
- Published studies on strike position and timbre for membranes, which is the physical justification for the position axis.
- Studies on hi-hat openness as a continuum and on cymbal choke.
- Snare-wire coupling studies, which justify the mechanism axis.
- Brush versus stick excitation studies.
- Any acoustics work that quantifies how many perceptually distinct strike positions or openness levels a listener can actually resolve - this bounds how many anchors the openness scale needs.

## Note on this bucket

This bucket answers a question no other bucket can: which of the distinctions in the model are physically real and perceptually resolvable, and which are marketing.
