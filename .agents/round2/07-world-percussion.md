# Bucket 07 — World and regional percussion nomenclature

Read `.agents/round2/BRIEF.md` first. It defines the three-round method, the axis
model and the output structure. Follow it exactly.

## What this bucket must cover

- Afro-Cuban: conga strokes - open, muff/mute, slap open and closed, bass, heel-toe/manoteo, tip, palm, glissando/moose call - and the drum names quinto, conga/tres golpes, tumba, salidor. Bongo: martillo, and the macho/hembra naming. Timbales: paila/cascara, abanico, rim, shell. Clave, guiro, chekere, cajon.
- Brazilian: surdo (open, muffled, marcacao), pandeiro (thumb, tip, heel, slap, shake), repinique, tamborim (virado), agogo, cuica, caixa, ganza, reco-reco, zabumba.
- Indian: tabla bols - na, ta, tin, tun, ge/ghe, ka/ke, dha, dhin, ti, te, re - and the dayan/bayan naming; mridangam and ghatam stroke names.
- Arabic, Turkish and Persian: doumbek/darbuka - doum, tek, ka, slap, pa - riq, bendir, daf, tar, zarb/tombak stroke names including the finger-snap and roll techniques.
- West African: djembe - bass, tone, slap - dunun family (dununba, sangban, kenkeni) with bell, sabar, talking drum.
- East Asian: Japanese taiko strokes and drum names (nagado, shime, okedo); Chinese opera percussion; Korean janggu and samulnori; Indonesian gamelan instrument names.
- Flamenco cajon and Peruvian cajon stroke names.

## Note on this bucket

These are living, codified vocabularies with centuries of pedagogy behind them. Get the stroke names from teaching material and from ethnomusicological sources, not from sample library marketing.
