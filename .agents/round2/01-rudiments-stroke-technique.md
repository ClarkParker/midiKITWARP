# Bucket 01 — Rudiments and stroke technique

Read `.agents/round2/BRIEF.md` first. It defines the three-round method, the axis
model and the output structure. Follow it exactly.

## What this bucket must cover

- PAS 40 International Drum Rudiments and the NARD 26 that preceded them - the official lists, their exact names and their historical revisions.
- Swiss Basler Trommel rudiment nomenclature, which is older than the American lists and names strokes the American lists do not.
- Stroke-type systems: full / down / tap / up stroke; the Moeller technique; the Gladstone technique; Freehand and push-pull. Name who codified each and where.
- The multiple-bounce family: buzz roll, press roll, closed roll, open roll, double-stroke roll, and the numbered rolls (5, 7, 9, 13, 17 stroke) - what distinguishes each PHYSICALLY.
- Grace-note ornaments: flam, flam tap, flam accent, flamacue, drag, ruff, single and double drag tap, ratamacue, and the Swiss equivalents. Give attack counts.
- Sticking systems and their notation: alternating, paradiddle family, hand-to-hand, lead hand, and how L/R sticking is written.
- Rimshot vocabulary as drummers actually use it: rimshot, half rimshot, gock, ping shot, stick shot, back stick, rim click, cross stick, side stick - and who uses which word.
- Brush technique vocabulary: sweep, swirl, stir, tap, slap, circular, figure-eight, Clayton Cameron's and Ed Thigpen's published terminology.
- Foot technique: heel-down, heel-up, heel-toe, slide, swivel; hi-hat foot chick, foot splash, and what a 'splash' means on a hi-hat versus a splash cymbal.

## Note on this bucket

Percussive Arts Society, drum method books, drum corps sources, Modern Drummer, conservatory percussion curricula.
