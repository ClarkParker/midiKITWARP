## 1. back-stick — a direct contradiction between two buckets
Bucket 01 reported `back-stick` as "unattested in any source reached".
Bucket 08 then made it the best-sourced term in its bucket:
  - Joe Marrella, "The Baron of Backsticking", Drum Corps World Vol. 36 No. 15, Dec 2007:
    backsticking developed 1938 as a left-hand training method, first taught to the USAF
    snare section in 1958 by John Dowlan.
  - World Drum Corps Hall of Fame, Dowlan bio: Osmond Post Cadets 1935; selected 1957 by
    M/Sgt Truman Crawford for the USAF Drum Corps.
  - Mirsky overturns the 1938 date with an artefact: Armstrong & Co. lithograph,
    "A.R. Carrington, champion drum soloist, 1870s", NYPL call no. PC MUSIC-Dru, Digital
    ID 832408, showing the flip mid-motion; corroborated by a Utica New York Observer
    review of 3 July 1878 describing flip and stick tosses in one sentence.
  => The term reaches back ~150 years. The reconciliation must record bucket 08's evidence
     and note that bucket 01's negative was scope-limited, not a refutation.

## 2. rim shot — entry into print bracketed
Ray Bauduc, Dixieland Drumming (1937), archive.org `RayBauducDixielandDrumming`,
unrestricted. Uses "rim shot" twice with no definition, as an already-established word,
and as a DYNAMIC ACCENT device rather than a timbre. Absent from Bower 1912, Gardner 1918
and Straight 1922 => entry into print between 1922 and 1937. Explains why Read 1953 found
no Italian/French/German equivalent while "muffled" had eight synonyms.
Krupa 1938 is NOT on archive.org, so the shaft-between-head-and-rim definition stays
UNVERIFIED.

## 3. A lineage caution that changes attestation counting
Peinkofer & Tannigel's English translation is by Kurt and Else Stone. So Stone 1980 and
Peinkofer/Tannigel 1976 are ONE lineage: their agreement is not corroboration. Any
attestation count that treats them as two independent traditions is wrong.
